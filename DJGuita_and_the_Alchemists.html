What the numbers say

  • Brujeria is the engine. It did twenty times Angel Face's streams in the same fourteen days, with no ad money behind it. Everything in this plan leads with it.
  • Spotify's algorithm already likes it. Seven of every ten Brujeria streams came from Spotify's own programming. You cannot buy that. You can feed it: saves, playlist adds and repeat listens are what the algorithm reads, and Brujeria's listeners are doing all three (9% added it to a playlist, 6% saved it). The job is to raise streams per listener from 1.5 toward 2 and above.
  • Your audience is the Spanish-speaking world first. Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Chile, Peru and Spain together with the US Latino cities (San Dimas, Fontana and Los Angeles in LA County; Dallas; Chicago) are about half of Brujeria's listening. Brazil is a strong third market on its own (São Paulo, Curitiba, Porto Alegre). Your top cities are Istanbul, Mexico City, Warsaw, São Paulo, Cairo, Santiago and Bogotá.
  • Three surprise markets the algorithm found for you: Turkey (433 streams, Istanbul your second city), Poland (342, Warsaw fourth) and Egypt (136, Cairo sixth). Nobody targeted them. They are worth a cheap test.
  • The paid campaign bought little. Angel Face's non-programmed streams, the ones an ad can produce, total about 110 in fourteen days. Whatever Aura charged, that is the ceiling of what it delivered; the campaign dashboard has the exact cost per listener. Paid promotion on a song the algorithm is not carrying is money spent against the current.
  • A release lifts the whole catalog. On October 11, the day after the double release, the original Time Flies jumped from near zero to 241 streams, almost all from Spotify's programming, with no promotion at all. Each new release re-introduces you to your followers and the algorithm. Cadence is a lever.
  • The country version found its own crowd. Time Flies (Country) is up 826% on the month, with the US at 35% of it, then Mexico, Brazil, Australia and Canada: a country-leaning audience the algorithm paired it with after October 18.
  • What the dashboard does not show yet: age and gender. That sits on the Audience tab, which was not in this read. It fills in the next pass.
  • The money, plainly. At the rough industry average of a few tenths of a cent per stream, Brujeria's fortnight is tens of dollars, not hundreds. Your DistroKid statements are the truth on that. A reliable income near $5,000 a month means well over a million streams a month across the catalog. That is a 100-fold climb from here, and it comes from compounding releases and the algorithm, not from bought playlist spots.

3The Dreamline, end to end

Dovahkiin's Meta-Dreamline is one pipeline from your Suno stems to a listener's thumb, run by the engine crew, approved by you at each gate. "Meta" means two things here: it runs on Meta's platforms where your listeners are, and it makes one piece of content into many platform-shaped posts.

Source

Your songs, stems and art live in your DJGuita folder. You drop; the crew notices. Masters stay yours.

Release

DistroKid delivers everywhere. The release calendar is yours to set; we prepare each release the way DistroKid wants it.

Listen

Every morning the crew reads Spotify for Artists, DistroKid, YouTube and Meta into one place: MUSEDASH.

Learn

Which countries, cities and ages are listening, which songs get saved, which playlists carry you. Your country stats already point at your market.

Make

Short clips cut from what works: lyric cards, the Brujeria animation (your "I Dream of Jeannie" homage), reels sized for Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook and Pinterest.

Place

Posts and Meta ads go out from your own accounts, on a budget you set. Money flows to the clips and countries that convert, and away from the ones that do not.

Measure

Cost per new listener, saves, follows, playlist adds, streams per dollar. Each week you see what every dollar bought.

Repeat

The crew proposes next week's moves. You approve, change or decline from your phone. Then it runs again.

What "autonomous" means in practice: the reading, cutting, scheduling and measuring run without you. Spending and releasing wait for your yes. That is the gate you asked for and it stays.

6The path forward, from the data

The numbers above set the order. Lead with Brujeria, feed the algorithm that is already carrying it, aim the paid money at the markets the data names, and release again soon.

  • This week: the Día de Muertos window. Brujeria is a witchcraft song and Mexico is your second country. November 1 and 2 are the natural moment. Spanish-language reels and lyric clips cut from the cover art and your animation concept, posted daily through November 2 on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, aimed at Mexico, the US Latino cities and Colombia. No camera needed.
  • Feed the algorithm, on purpose. Every post and ad asks for one of three actions: save it, add it to your playlist, follow DJGuita. Those are the signals Spotify's programming reads. Streams per listener is the scoreboard number for this: 1.5 today, 2.0 is the first target.
  • Paid ads, your own account, three small tests. Instagram and Facebook reels placements from your Meta Business account, about $10 to $20 a day each, two weeks: (1) Mexico and the US Latino cities in Spanish; (2) Brazil with Portuguese captions; (3) the surprise markets, Turkey and Poland, with the animation and no words. Each test is read off the dashboard the same way: the ad's streams land in the non-programmed sources, so the cost per listener is visible and honest. The winner gets the budget; the losers stop.
  • Spotify's own promotion tools. Spotify for Artists has paid in-app promotion (Showcase and Marquee in the Campaigns tab, where your account qualifies). They convert inside Spotify, which feeds the algorithm directly, and they are the one paid product that is not a middle-man. We check your eligibility on the dashboard and price one Brujeria test against the Meta tests.
  • Pitch the next release to Spotify's editors, free. Spotify for Artists lets you pitch an unreleased song to its editorial playlists at least seven days before release. Demons In My Closet goes in that form with the data in hand: Brujeria's countries, its playlist adds, its 70% programmed share. That is the pitch editors read.
  • Release cadence: the next song inside six weeks. October 11 proved that a release lifts the whole catalog through Release Radar and your followers. Demons In My Closet, then the Brujeria animated video as its own event, keep the algorithm coming back. Angel Face gets re-cut on your schedule, not promoted as it stands.
  • Stop paying against the current. No more paid campaigns on a song the algorithm is not carrying, and no bought playlist placements. Vendors mid-contract run to the end of their term while we measure them; nothing is cancelled on a hunch, it is replaced on a number.
  • Radio money. Register with ASCAP or BMI and SoundExchange this month. Time Flies is already on air and that money is uncollected until you do.
  • The weekly read. Every Monday MUSEDASH shows streams, listeners, streams per listener, saves, playlist adds, the programmed share, the top five countries and what each ad dollar bought. You approve the next week's moves from your phone.

7The rules, yours

  • Your masters stay yours. No points on masters for contract work; flat fees or a capped test deal, as you did with Alex.
  • Nothing is released, posted as an ad, or spent without your yes.
  • The going rate you know, about 20% of a song for a producer, is the ceiling, not the floor.
  • Your friends-and-family policy is yours to set. The plan does not depend on anyone's favors.
  • You do not have to be on camera. The engine cuts from art, lyrics and animation; you appear when you choose to.

Brujeria earned its own listeners. The Dreamline's job is to find the next ten thousand of them and keep every dollar of it in your hands.
